Automated checks
    Rows held back
    Refreshing it
    1. Export sales into the template columns: month (YYYY-MM), sku, name, category, amazon_price, list_price, units, cogs, freight, amz_fulfil. One row per listing per month drives the trends and standings.
    2. Load it with Load a sales export (switched off in this demo). The checks run first; bad rows are held back and listed here with the reason.
    3. Every page and the meeting deck rebuild from the clean rows. Nobody copies numbers into slides.

    The file is read in the browser and never uploaded anywhere. In production this would run from Vendor Central reports on a schedule, or a Power BI dataflow, instead of a manual file.

    Where every number comes from
    Listings generated
    Thirteen listings for Lakeshore Wellness, a fictional consumer-products vendor, across four product families. The model treats Amazon as the seller on each, which is what a 1P vendor relationship looks like from the shopper side.
    Prices generated
    An Amazon price and a list price per listing, set to the shape real 1P catalogs show: most items a little under list, a few deep under it. Revenue applies that one price to the full year; a live version would use the realized average selling price from Vendor Central, since deals and Subscribe & Save move it.
    What Amazon pays us estimate
    The price each listing actually sells for, less the trade discount on the left. This assumes the cost price follows the shelf price; in a real 1P deal it is set by contract, so a deep Amazon discount would compress Amazon's margin rather than ours. Amazon's strikethrough list price is shown for context but not used, since on several listings it's a reference price well above where the item sells.
    CRaP risk line assumption
    A listing counts as at risk when Amazon keeps under 15% of its price after its fulfillment cost. Amazon's real threshold isn't public.
    Unit volumes assumption
    Set per listing so the catalog has a few fast movers and a long tail, the way a real one does. Adjustable in the left panel.
    Monthly history sample
    Twelve months shaped by each category's season, to show the trend and standings views. Replaced by any export with a month column.
    Ranges assumption
    A Monte Carlo on the Profitability tab reruns the model 2,000 times, varying the trade discount, allowances, advertising, product cost, volume, fulfillment and freight. The spreads it uses are stated on the tile and are assumptions, not measured.
    What we keep definition
    Fully loaded: after allowances, advertising, freight, product cost including allocated plant and floor space, and an inventory carrying charge. Plant and floor space is fixed, so for keep-or-cut decisions on a listing, look at the margin before that line in its cost build. Margins are shown on invoiced sales; on GAAP net sales, after allowances, they read higher.
    Product cost estimate
    Assumed manufacturing costs for molded plastic, wipes and rubber goods, split into materials, labor, packaging, and plant and floor space. The ear plugs are costed as purchased goods, to show how a sourced line sits beside a manufactured one.
    Star ratings generated
    A rating and a rating count per listing, generated to track volume the way they do on real listings: the fast movers carry the big counts.
    Review text sample
    Written to exercise the classifier, not real customer reviews. The classifier itself is real and runs the same way on an actual review export.
    Amazon's fulfillment estimate
    Amazon's incremental cost to pick, pack and ship each item, tiered by size, since small items usually ship in multi-item orders. Used only to judge whether a listing is profitable for Amazon, alongside the co-op and allowances Amazon receives.
