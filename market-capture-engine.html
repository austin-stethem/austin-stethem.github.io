1. The Problem: You Cannot Defend a Market You Cannot See

The offensive half of the portfolio The five-step pricing arc elsewhere on this site is defensive — it protects margin against a cost shock. This is the other half. Every dollar it identifies is revenue we did not previously have, taken from a competitor at the moment they are least able to hold it.

Competitor location data at rooftop accuracy, across the operating footprint, for a fragmented industry of national chains, regional banners, co-op affiliates and independents, is not something you can buy. It has to be built. And the reason to build it is not curiosity about where competitors are — it is that consolidation in this industry is constant, and every consolidation event orphans customers who have to choose a new supplier under time pressure.

When two chains merge, overlapping stores close. The customers of a closing store do not evaluate the market calmly; they need parts that week. Whoever is already in the door wins the account, and whoever arrives after the sign changes is negotiating against an incumbent. The entire value of this system is compressing the time between a merger is announced and our representative is standing in the affected shop.

2. The Harvest: Building the Dataset That Doesn't Exist

Locations are harvested per brand from mapping APIs using a coordinate grid across the operating footprint, supplemented by parsed location exports. Each brand runs as its own pipeline so that a failure in one does not contaminate the others, and results are filtered to the operating states using an anchored pattern on the state-and-postcode segment of the formatted address rather than a naive substring search — a distinction that matters when a state abbreviation also appears inside street names.

Four Passes to Rooftop Accuracy

The first version of this dataset was not usable, and the reason is worth stating plainly: geocoders do not fail loudly. Asked for an address they cannot resolve, they return the town centroid with a success code, and every store in that town lands on the same pin. Getting to rooftop precision took four passes, each tightening the tolerance:

  1. Centroid and street, usable for regional density, useless for trade-area analysis.
  2. Rooftop and street, better, but silently mixed precision levels in a single column.
  3. Street within 100 feet, close enough to be dangerous; still placed some stores on the wrong side of a divided highway.
  4. Full rooftop — the current standard. 3,768 competitor locations across ten brand families, alongside roughly 32,000 customer ship-to points covering roughly 27,000 accounts, spanning eight states.

A separate insight was required for the customer side. Business-to-business billing addresses are frequently post office boxes or corporate headquarters hundreds of miles from the shop that actually receives the parts. Geocoding the billing street produces a confident, precise, entirely wrong coordinate. The pipeline therefore geocodes the business name plus city and state, deliberately discarding the street address, and then applies a 75-mile proximity shield to catch the ones that still resolve to a head office.

3. Integrity: The Pipeline Is a Loop, Not a Line

What made this dataset usable was one decision: treat a missing address as a finding, not a blank. A geocoder handed an address it cannot resolve does not fail. It returns the centre of the town, plus a confidence score nobody reads. Left alone, those rows put competitor stores miles from where they stand, and at this scale the map still looks right. That is the dangerous part. Everything downstream drinks from this file: the sales organisation working an account list, the executive reading a threat map, the logistics review. None of them would catch it. So the rescue ladder below makes the failures countable.

A scraped dataset has a specific failure mode: it is never empty and never obviously broken. It is quietly wrong in ways that survive inspection. The controls below exist because each of these failures actually occurred, and the pipeline runs as a repeating detect-and-repair cycle rather than a single clean pass.

  • Quarantine, never drop Addresses that fail to resolve are written to a holding file rather than discarded, so the failure count is visible on every run instead of vanishing into a smaller output. failed_geocodes.csv
  • Rescue pass Quarantined records are re-attempted through alternate geocoders and reformatted address variants. What resolves is merged back into the master set. quarantine_rescue_engine → rescue_quarantine.py → merge_rescued_data.py
  • Documented degradation Records that survive every attempt fall back to a city centroid; but the fallback is recorded as such, so nobody downstream mistakes it for a rooftop coordinate. A known-bad-locality list exists because certain towns fail repeatedly. city_center_fallbacks.csv · problem_children_towns.csv
  • Imposter detection A brand-signature check cross-validates the returned business name against the brand the query was issued under. A generic query returns lookalike independents and rival banners; multi-alias signatures resolve co-op affiliates and acquired banners to their true parent. purge_imposters.py
  • Reconciling rather than deleting A name-versus-brand disagreement usually means a real competitor was returned under the wrong query and filed under the wrong banner, not that the record is fake. Deleting it removes a genuine location from the census; correcting the brand label keeps it. The distinction matters because the harvester deduplicates on provider identity across every brand it queries, so a misfiled store may be the only copy of that store in the dataset. reclassify, don't drop
  • Alias collisions with place names Word-boundary matching stops a brand token firing inside a longer word, but it cannot stop an alias that is also the name of a town; and business listings routinely carry a city suffix. Any alias sharing a name with a locality in the footprint will match every rival store in that town, so those patterns are constrained rather than trusted, and the reconciliation step above is what makes a false match survivable. signature scoping
  • The inverse test Cross-validation catches contamination between known brands. It cannot catch a wholly unrelated business returned under a brand query, because that name matches no signature at all and nothing fires. The complementary rule closes it: a record whose name matches none of its own assigned brand's aliases is quarantined for review rather than trusted. That is the check that finds the true false positives. quarantine on no-match
  • Deduplication and reinjection Coordinate collisions and repeat identifiers are collapsed, and records recovered by earlier stages are reintegrated into the master file. Dedupe_2.py · data_re-integration.py · master_injector.py
  • Entity resolution across systems Branch identifiers arrive in different shapes from the point-of-sale extract and the location master. Prefixes and leading zeros are normalised to a clean key before any join, because a silent join failure removes an entire branch from the map. Clean_ID normalisation
The principle underneath all of them

A record that fails should cost you a line in a log file, not a row in the dataset. Silent deletion is the failure mode that makes a scraped dataset dangerous, because the output still looks complete. Every stage here reports what it removed and where it went.

The dataset this runs on did not exist, and no vendor was going to sell it to us at a price anyone would approve. I built it, and I made the argument in those terms, not fund a data initiative, but I can build this from data we already own, and what I need from you is a monthly snapshot and a decision about who acts on it.

What the Representative Actually Receives

Field users do not get this dashboard. They get a stripped-down build that outputs the hitlist above and nothing else — ranked accounts, the market, the distance from their hub, and the reason the account is in play. That separation is deliberate: a rep needs a call list and a talking point, not a model. The same engine serves both audiences because the analysis is identical; only the surface differs.

5. Four Functions, One Map

A merger is the most dramatic use of this dataset and the least frequent one. Consolidation events arrive every few years; the map is consulted every week, by four different functions asking four different questions of the same underlying geometry.

Function The question they bring What the map returns
Executive Where does a competitor's restructuring create an opening? Closure candidates, the accounts attached to them, and the branch positioned to absorb the volume. Episodic, and high value when it lands.
Sales management Where should representatives be spending their time? Contested pockets ranked by exposure. An account with three rivals inside a ten-minute drive warrants a different call frequency to one with none, and that distinction is invisible in a customer list sorted by revenue.
Marketing Where does promotional spend actually move something? The geography of contestability. Spending to defend an uncontested trade area buys nothing; the same spend inside a contested pocket is the difference between holding an account and losing it.
Pricing Where can we hold price, and where must we concede it? Regional price positioning, and account-level discretion targeted at accounts inside a threat radius rather than granted across the board.

The Pricing Use Is the One That Pays for It

Sales deployment and promotional targeting are the visible applications. The one that recovers the most margin is quieter: using contestability to decide where discretion is warranted and, more importantly, where it is not.

Discounting is usually granted account by account, in response to pressure, by whoever is closest to the customer. That produces exactly the dispersion documented in the defensive track, hundreds of prices for one part, none of them the product of a deliberate policy. The map converts that into something bounded. An account sitting inside a dense competitive pocket has real leverage, and a defensive concession there is a considered decision. An account with no competitor within a twenty-minute drive does not, and a discount granted there is margin handed over against a threat that does not exist.

The same figure therefore answers two questions in opposite directions. Read offensively, it says where somebody else's customers are reachable. Read defensively, it says where our own are exposed and what a concession is actually buying. That is why the entitlement audit in the final step of this track uses this score as a constraint rather than a target; and why a competitive map built to find opportunities ends up governing how much margin the business gives away.

Three Further Tools on the Same Foundation

Beyond those four consumers, the same foundation carries three further tools already in the field:

  • Delivery overlap mapping. Branch-to-customer polylines reveal where two branches are servicing the same town, where routes double back, and where delivery vehicles are being worked harder than the revenue justifies.
  • Territory balancing. Representative accounts plotted against their hub store expose reps carrying too wide a radius and reps under-tasked relative to their patch, with account-level and city-level overlap reports isolating where two reps are calling on the same customer.
  • Acquisition targeting. Independent shops whose owners are approaching retirement without a succession plan are frequently unwilling to sell to a large chain. Mapping them against our footprint supports a white-glove approach in which the owner typically stays on as manager of the location.

6. Phase 2: From Proximity To Revenue At Risk

The deployed system answers where is the competition. The next question is which of our accounts are most likely to leave, and what is that worth; and this is design work, not shipped work. It is included because the constraint on building it is data collection rather than modelling, and knowing which is which is most of the job.

The scoring output should be denominated in gross profit at risk, not an abstract threat level. A dimensionless score cannot be prioritised, summed, or defended in a budget conversation. Dollars rank themselves.

How I Would Know If It Worked

Churn labels derived from transaction history are the validation set. If accounts that actually defected do not score higher than accounts that stayed, the weights are wrong and that is discoverable before deployment not after. This also removes the dependence on field reports as ground truth, representatives report where they are losing and stay quiet where they are comfortable, so tip-offs are a biased channel. They corroborate; they should not calibrate.

What This Case Study Is Actually About

Not scraping. Scraping is the easy part and always has been. The work is everything between a raw API response and a number an executive will act on: knowing that a geocoder lies quietly rather than failing loudly, that a business-to-business billing address is usually not where the parts go, that a proximity pair is not a store closure, and that thirty miles means one thing in a metro and something else entirely in a farming county.

None of those are technical problems. They are the difference between a map that looks impressive and a map someone is willing to deploy a sales force against.

The Defensive Half

This system finds revenue that isn't ours yet. The five-step pricing arc handles the other side of the ledger: mapping competitive exposure across the branch network, then defending blended margin through a vendor cost shock without conceding the Key Value Items customers price-check us on.
